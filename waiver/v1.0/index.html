1. What EMS training is

EMS (electrical muscle stimulation) training uses a suit with electrodes connected to a device (miha bodytec) that sends electrical impulses to the muscles while I do simple exercises. The impulses make my muscles contract much harder than during the same exercises without EMS. A session usually lasts about 20 minutes of EMS training, followed by about 10 minutes of recovery (for example, a massage program, stretching or treadmill walking). My trainer supervises me the whole time and sets the intensity, which I can ask to lower or stop at any moment.

EMS training is a fitness service. It is not medical treatment, physical therapy or rehabilitation, and it is not meant to diagnose, treat or cure any condition. My trainer is a fitness professional, not a doctor.

2. Risks of EMS training (informed consent)

I understand that EMS training, like any intense exercise, has risks. They include, but are not limited to:

  • Muscle soreness for several days, which can be stronger than after regular training, especially after the first sessions.
  • Rhabdomyolysis: a rare but serious condition in which damaged muscle tissue releases substances into the blood that can harm the kidneys. The risk is higher if a session is too intense, too frequent, or if I am dehydrated, ill, or take certain medications. Warning signs include dark (brown or cola-colored) urine, severe or unusual muscle pain, swelling or weakness. If I notice any of these, I will seek medical care right away and tell Mission U.
  • Skin irritation, redness or burns where the electrodes touch the skin.
  • Dizziness, nausea, fainting, low blood pressure or fatigue, during or after a session.
  • Strains, sprains, falls and other injuries from the exercises, the equipment, the floor, the treadmill, or moving around the studio.
  • Worsening of an existing health condition, including heart, blood pressure, nerve or muscle conditions, whether or not I know about it.
  • Interference with electronic or metal implants in the body.
  • In very rare cases, heart problems or other serious medical emergencies, which can lead to permanent disability or death.

I have had the chance to ask questions about these risks, and any questions I asked were answered to my satisfaction.

3. My health and the contraindications

I have read the list of contraindications below. I understand that it is not a complete medical list and that only a doctor can tell me whether EMS training is safe for me.

Absolute contraindications. EMS training is not permitted if any of these apply to me:

  • Pacemaker or other electronic implants
  • Metal implants in the stimulation area
  • Epilepsy
  • Pregnancy
  • Recent surgery (less than 3 months ago)

Relative contraindications. Written approval from my doctor is required before training if any of these apply to me:

  • Cardiovascular diseases, arrhythmia, hypertension
  • Asthma
  • Cancer (current or past)
  • Inguinal hernias
  • Varicose veins, thrombosis
  • Diabetes
  • Neurological or autoimmune disorders
  • Recent injuries or surgeries
  • Open wounds, inflammation, burns, or skin conditions in the stimulation area

I promise that:

  1. My answers on the health questions in this form are true and complete. Mission U relies on them to decide whether and how to train me.
  2. If any absolute contraindication applies to me now or later, I will not do EMS training.
  3. If any relative contraindication applies to me, I will bring written approval from my doctor before training, and I will follow any limits my doctor sets.
  4. Before every session I will tell my trainer about any change in my health: for example a new diagnosis, pregnancy, surgery, injury, new medication, illness or fever.
  5. I will follow my trainer's instructions before, during and after a session, including what to drink and eat before training.
  6. I will tell my trainer immediately if I feel pain, discomfort, dizziness, burning, or anything unusual, and I understand that I can stop the session at any time.

I understand that Mission U may refuse, pause or stop training me at any time if it believes training may not be safe for me, or if I have not provided the required doctor's approval.
