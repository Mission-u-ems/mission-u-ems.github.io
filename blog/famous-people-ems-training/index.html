Which famous people train with EMS? We checked the sources

Lists of “celebrities who love EMS” are everywhere. Most of them have no source at all. We kept only the names backed by the person’s own words or by major media.

Our rule for this article was simple: every name needs a reliable source. That means an interview, the person’s own words on a show or on social media, or a report in major media. A studio’s “our famous clients” page does not count.

Here is who made the list, and what exactly they did.

Tom Holland: the Spider-Man suit under the suit

On The Graham Norton Show, Tom Holland described EMS training as part of his preparation for Spider-Man. Men’s Health quoted him: “You wear this black suit and they put these pads on you and you basically do a yoga class while they electrocute you.”1 He added: “It’s a lot of fun and it works but it really is the worst thing I’ve ever done.”

USA TODAY repeated it in 2025: Holland “said he did EMS training while preparing to play Spider-Man.”2 In 2026, Elle also listed EMS as part of his movie prep.3

Kendall and Kylie Jenner: on camera

The sisters tried EMS on camera, on their family’s reality show. According to USA TODAY, Kendall and Kylie Jenner “did an EMS workout in an episode of ‘The Kardashians.’”2

Sarah Michelle Gellar: twice a week, 20 minutes

Of all the names here, Gellar has talked about EMS the most. In 2025 she told Parade about her EMS vest: “You can only do it twice a week for about 20 minutes, so it cuts down the time you spend on your workout.” She also said she still works with a trainer for strength training and loves Pilates.4

In 2026 an Elle editor wrote that Gellar had told her in an interview that EMS was “one of her new favorite things”.3 Her routine became so talked about that The Conversation published an explainer by a university researcher on the trend.5

Chrissy Teigen: “It’s so good, honestly”

In April 2021 Chrissy Teigen showed an EMS session in her Instagram stories. The Zoe Report quoted her: “It’s so good, honestly.”6

Later, on The Kelly Clarkson Show, she was more direct about the day after: “My whole butt hurts so bad just sitting down.”2 She also compared 20 minutes in the suit to four hours of working out. Elle noted it is hard to tell how much of that is hyperbole, and so do we.3

Cristiano Ronaldo: a brand ambassador, not a suit client

Ronaldo’s name comes up a lot, so let’s be precise. Since 2015 he has been the face of SIXPAD, a Japanese line of EMS devices. Engadget described it at launch as “a muscle-stimulation device that promises to make your abs look like those on his body.”7 In the campaign Ronaldo said: “When I look at this product, I see myself.”8 In 2023 the maker, MTG, was still releasing products “supported by Cristiano Ronaldo”.9

That is a paid partnership for a home device for the abs and core. It does not show that EMS is behind his physique, and it is not whole-body suit training.

Who is not on this list

You may have seen Kim Kardashian, famous footballers or whole football clubs on lists like this. We could not find a reliable source that confirms they train with EMS, so we left them out. If a reliable source appears, we will add it.

Famous fans don’t prove that a method works. What matters is a good program, a careful trainer and consistency.

What this means for you

EMS is popular with busy people for a simple reason: a session is short, and the suit works many muscles at once. The researcher writing in The Conversation put it well: EMS “should be viewed as a supplement, not a substitute, for regular exercise”.5

That is how we use it at Mission U: a private 20-minute session with a trainer, the intensity adjusted to how you feel, as part of a healthy routine.
